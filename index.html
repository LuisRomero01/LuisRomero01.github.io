Ethics Discussion (Post Two)

For our classes ethics discussion, I talked about the area I grew up in and how the diversity was able to shape who I am today and how I make decisions. One thing I forgot to mention was how being an uncle has also been a big influence in my life. Ive been an uncle since I was in the fifth grade, so majority of my life has been being told to be a role model for my nieces and nephews. This new responsibility made me think a lot about my decisions and motivated me to pursue goals that may seem hard, just so I can hopefully be a person my younger family members can look up to, same way my brother, dad and uncles did for me.

I enjoyed having the ethics discussion in class because it made me realize we are pretty much the same when it comes to deciding how to treat people, how we make decisions, and our overall morals. One specific discussion that stood out to me was the “would you go back in time to kill baby hitler”, not only because it was funny, but because one person made a good point that we already know that hitler eventually dies and World War Two comes to an end, but if we were to take out hitler, we might open the door for another person to take the same place and possibly not loose. This funny yet though provoking discussion reminded me what my mom used to tell me as a kid, “things happen for a reason, good or bad, its how you react that makes you who you are”.

Blog Post One

My best “in-class” computing experience has to be working on the projects for my AI class. I consider this one of my favorite experiences because it made me feel like an actual scientist. I was creating charts, comparing results with my peers, and adjusting models to get the best results. Although the class was a bit tedious, I still enjoyed the stress of having a neural network take forever to load because my laptop only had 8 GB of RAM at the time.

My best “outside-of-class” experience was participating in the 2026 Hackathon at App. This was probably one of the first times I truly felt like a software developer. My friends and I created a web service that made it easier for students, faculty, and visitors to find their lost items by providing a website that tracks lost items turned in to different buildings. I got to work on the backend side of the project, and I really enjoyed planning which frameworks, databases, and features we were going to use. Luckily, we ended up winning the “Most Practical” award and got some Nerds Clusters as a prize.
